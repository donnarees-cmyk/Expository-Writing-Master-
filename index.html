Florida Target Topic: Aquifers & Springs

Source 1: The Invisible Giant - Florida’s Aquifer

Nonfiction Article

Beneath the sand, pine flatwoods, and bustling cities of Florida lies one of the world's most productive underground water systems: the Floridan Aquifer. Spanning over 100,000 square miles across Florida and parts of neighboring states, this underground reservoir consists of porous limestone rock layers filled with fresh water.

Rainfall soaks into the soil and slowly filters downward through layers of sand and limestone in a process called recharge. This natural filtration removes sediments and impurities, producing remarkably clean freshwater that supplies drinking water for over 90% of Florida's residents.

Source 2: Windows to the Deep - Florida Springs

Informational Guide

Florida is home to more than 1,000 natural freshwater springs, representing the greatest concentration of first-magnitude springs on Earth. Springs occur where pressurized groundwater from the Floridan Aquifer forces its way up to the surface through natural openings in the limestone rock.

Because the spring water maintains a constant year-round temperature of approximately 72 degrees Fahrenheit (22°C), these crystal-clear basins serve as vital winter sanctuaries for West Indian manatees seeking warmth when ocean temperatures drop. However, human development and excessive water pumping pose significant threats to these ecosystems.
